## I. Putin Sets the Table, Xi Hosts — the Ghost of a "New Yalta"

![Putin proposes a US-China-Russia summit during APEC in Shenzhen this November, with Zelensky excluded](https://vien.wangshengfeng.dpdns.org/img/4120304303646237780/1.png)

### What happened

The *Wall Street Journal* reported exclusively: Russian President Vladimir Putin has proposed that he, US President Trump, and Chinese leader Xi Jinping hold a trilateral summit during the APEC meetings in **Shenzhen on November 18–19**, attempting to end the four-year Russia-Ukraine war through great-power negotiation — **with Ukrainian President Zelensky completely excluded**.

According to people familiar with the matter, Putin floated the idea in an October 9 phone call with Trump, in which the two also agreed that the US would buy Russian diesel and help sell it on global markets. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov confirmed: "All three sides recognize the possibility of such a meeting, but there is no clear framework yet for the meeting itself or a potential agenda."

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio confirmed during a visit to Uzbekistan that the US side is discussing it. A senior US official said Trump has not given final agreement but, "given how badly Trump wants to deliver on his campaign promise to end the war, he is open to it." Ukraine's foreign minister has flatly rejected any summit held without Kyiv's participation.

### Xiao Wang's take: Putin's four calculations

**One, laundering the narrative:** recast an "aggressive war" as "great powers negotiating over security concerns," turning Zelensky from "defender of sovereignty" into "obstacle to peace."

**Two, splitting the US and Europe:** precisely exploiting the rift between Trump and Europe. Keep Europe out of the room, bring China in — and the transatlantic crack becomes a canyon.

**Three, rejoining the great-power club:** using the "end the war" bait to bargain for sanctions relief, G8 readmission, and an end to international isolation.

**Four, forcing Beijing to take a public stand** — the most poisonous move. If Xi sits at the center of that table in Shenzhen, it amounts to endorsing the idea that might can redraw spheres of influence, and the "leader of the Global South" / "non-interference" moral brand he has spent years building would collapse overnight.

### Xi hosting: the risks dwarf the rewards

The upside: showcasing diplomatic stature, burnishing the "great power" narrative, and locking in an achievement of managing US-China differences.

But the risks are threefold: **escalating conflict with Europe** (the EU is pushing carbon tariffs and EV tariffs); **destroying the "Global South" halo** (how would Brazil, South Africa, and Indonesia see it?); and **carrying a historical stain** (hosting a stage where sovereignty gets sold off).

> A Taiwan perspective: if great powers can privately trade away Ukraine's sovereignty, Taipei has no reason to believe the Taiwan Strait won't be the next bargaining chip. Even more dangerous: if Xi tries to bundle in the Taiwan question, he will face the backlash of "internationalizing the Taiwan issue."

### The bankruptcy of "reverse Nixon" theory

Over the past year, some US commentary fantasized about Trump playing a masterstroke of "allying with Russia against China." Reality proves otherwise: **Trump has neither a clear strategic framework nor value-based convictions — his interactions with Putin and Xi are pure short-term transactional opportunism.** To this day, no one can say clearly what US policy toward Russia and China actually is.

## II. NYSE Takes Options Trading to 23 Hours

![NYSE extends options trading on 29 underlyings to 23 hours a day starting December 2026](https://vien.wangshengfeng.dpdns.org/img/4120304303646237780/2.png)

### What happened

The NYSE, following CBOE, announced it will extend options trading on 29 core underlyings (Nvidia, Bank of America, SPY, IWM, and others) to **7:30–9:25 AM and 4:00–4:15 PM Eastern**. Starting December 6, 2026, that means **23 hours a day, five days a week**.

### Xiao Wang's take: a pillow for institutions, a pit for retail

Good for institutions: after-hours earnings and geopolitical shocks can finally be hedged in real time instead of waiting for the open to blow up. A trap for retail: **pre- and post-market liquidity is paper-thin and volatility quotes are distorted — chasing rallies and panic-selling in those hours is essentially handing money to market makers.**

My view: options beginners (under two years of trading, don't understand the Greeks) should completely ignore the extended hours. Playing in them is walking into the pot pre-seasoned.

## III. Spies and Bans on the AI Battlefield

![Former OpenAI engineer accused of planting a listening device; US bans DeepSeek across federal agencies](https://vien.wangshengfeng.dpdns.org/img/4120304303646237780/3.png)

### What happened

**The OpenAI spy case:** former engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan (a Saudi national) was accused of secretly planting a recording device in an executive meeting room — discovered on September 30, arrested on October 9. **Prosecutors allege** he was in frequent contact with members of the Saudi royal family (note: Saudi government direction has not been confirmed).

**DHS bans DeepSeek comprehensively:** a Department of Homeland Security order requires federal civilian agencies to purge DeepSeek entirely (including third-party products built on it). The order was actually signed on July 24 but only made public on October 19.

### Xiao Wang's take: AI competition has gone full spy-thriller

The AI security threat has shifted from "a future destroying humanity" to the immediate **war of espionage and data sovereignty**. Governments care more about regime security than human security. The Saudi case shows even "allies" can't be trusted; the DeepSeek ban shows the US has finally learned "reciprocity" — China has been doing this all along.

The future belongs to "compliant, auditable, privately deployed" vertical models. Selling shovels (compliance consulting, private deployment) is a far surer bet than panning for gold (training foundation models).

## IV. The PBOC "Anchor Shift": a Rate Cut It Dares Not Name

![China's central bank studies shifting open-market operations to the overnight rate, an effective 15bp cut](https://vien.wangshengfeng.dpdns.org/img/4120304303646237780/4.png)

### What happened

PBOC Governor Pan Gongsheng announced at the Financial Street Forum: the bank is studying shifting the main tenor of open-market operations from **7 days to overnight**, making the overnight reverse-repo rate the main policy rate. The current 7-day reverse-repo rate is 1.40% versus 1.25% overnight — **the switch is a de facto 15-basis-point rate cut**.

### Xiao Wang's take: the most honest — and most helpless — choice

Why not just cut rates directly? A cut sends too strong a signal: the yuan comes under pressure, capital outflows, and the "stable expectations" story for real estate collapses. Why was the anchor shift unavoidable? The 7-day reverse-repo rate had become a "zombie rate" — banks don't borrow at 7 days anymore, so anchoring the LPR to it meant the transmission chain was already broken.

> The most ironic timing: the statistics bureau had just announced Q3 GDP of 4.5%, "everything is great," and the central bank immediately turned around and delivered a "technical rate cut." The central bank's books are far more honest than the statistics bureau's — it knows how cold the real economy is.

## V. The Deformity Behind 4.5%: Exports Can't Carry 1.4 Billion People Alone

![China's Q3 GDP grew 4.5% but fixed-asset investment collapsed 7.2% and consumption rose only 1.1%](https://vien.wangshengfeng.dpdns.org/img/4120304303646237780/5.png)

### What happened

The National Bureau of Statistics reported Q3 GDP growth of **4.5%** (versus 4.4% expected), with 4.6% for the first three quarters combined. But the structural data is shocking:

- **Fixed-asset investment: -7.2%** (January–September)
- **Real estate investment: -20.6%**
- **Retail sales: only +1.1%** (September)
- **September exports: +36.3%** in a single month (+16.4% cumulative for the first three quarters — note the different scopes)

Reuters and the WSJ both warned: "export prosperity is masking a deep domestic crisis."

### Xiao Wang's take: this isn't transition pain — it's demand-side collapse

An economy of 1.4 billion people, with investment collapsing and consumption stalled, running on exports alone — that's not sustainable. Exports earn foreign exchange, but they don't make it into ordinary people's paychecks and they don't solve unemployment and deflation.

The root cause: the property crash destroyed wealth, plus extreme anxiety about the future → low desire to spend + precautionary saving. Economists agree on the prescription: fiscal transfers to households and a social safety net.

But Xi is going the opposite way: **no fiscal backstop for households; instead, big-data back-audits are squeezing people to pay up social-security arrears**, and Shandong has even declared "individuals are the primary bearers of their own pension responsibility." That's not building confidence — it's wringing out life savings. "Fighting poison with poison" governance — absurd, but real.

The Japan-in-the-1990s feeling grows stronger: deflation → rate cuts → interest-rate arbitrage (UBS's panda bonds: borrowing renminbi at 1.69% and swapping into dollars to earn 5%+) → companies slashing prices abroad → domestic wages squeezed → even weaker domestic demand. A negative-feedback loop — Japan walked it for 30 years and still hasn't exited.
