Nov 15 Headlines: A $200 Million Options Lesson, a NATO Ticket and a Constitutional Notice — the Rules Are on Paper, but Who Enforces Them

November 15, 2026 · Read the original (Chinese)

Xiao Wang's Daily Finance · November 15, 2026 · Content period: November 14 · ~12 min read

Today's lineup: $196 million in options paper gains · Donbas for NATO · North Korea amends its constitution · eight disciplined in the gene-editing case

Hook: The Rules Are on Paper — Who Enforces Them?

Jensen Huang's scandal hasn't cooled yet, and the Financial Times has already done the maths: an unidentified trader spent $8.32 million on a set of put options on October 12, and by November 12 the position showed a paper gain of about $196 million — 24 times the money in. On the same day, Reuters reported that the US side had, for the first time at the negotiating table, linked NATO membership to territory in Donbas; North Korea's constitutional amendment went online in full, with South Korea named in the constitution for the first time as "another country bordering to the south"; and the official notice on the Shanghai Jiao Tong University gene-editing death also came out — seven disciplinary items, eight people involved.

On the surface these four stories have nothing to do with one another. Underneath them sits the same question. The rules are written on paper — who guarantees they are enforced?

What Happened

A 24x Options Trade

The Financial Times' Alphaville column ran a November 13 piece whose headline said it with a wink: "OK who just made $200mn shorting Nvidia ahead of Jensxit?" — so, who made $200 million shorting Nvidia before Jensen's exit? The maths comes from Joseph Niles of Macro Risk Advisors, checked line by line. The position was opened on October 12, 2026, when Nvidia stood at $226 (closing that day at $226.32): buy 180,000 put options struck at $150, expiring January 15, 2027, and at the same time sell 280,000 put options struck at $100; net cost $8.32 million; by November 12, with the share price at $166.19, the paper gain was about $196 million — roughly 24 times the money in.

But two things have to be said clearly first. First, the $196 million is a paper gain produced by a valuation model, not cash already in the bank. The trader has not been confirmed to have closed the position; the Financial Times only says he "can — and probably should — sell the contracts before expiry to lock in the gain." Second, the show's "the share price is around $165–166 now" is already out of date. At the close on November 13, Nvidia was $162.85, about $3 lower than the show said. The closer the price gets to $150, the more these options are worth.

A Negotiation With a Price Tag

Reuters reported on November 14 that, after the latest round of peace talks in Abu Dhabi, the US negotiating team put a hypothetical to the Ukrainian side: "If Ukraine joins NATO, would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?" Zelensky told reporters about it on November 13, calling it the first time in the talks that NATO membership had been linked to a territorial concession, and on that basis judging that "NATO is back on the agenda."

Zelensky's answer came in three layers: no readiness to give up territory; if four security guarantees were provided — concrete security assurances from NATO and the EU, a strong and well-funded army retained, long-term Western military and financial support, and Western funding for post-war reconstruction — he would put the question of ceding Donbas to a national referendum; but no arrangement would "recognise occupied territory as belonging to Russia."

But this is only Zelensky relaying an American "question/probe," not a confirmed formal US proposal, let alone a written plan. As of the morning of November 14, no US official had publicly confirmed or denied the swap, and the White House had said nothing publicly.

A Split Written Into the Constitution

North Korea passed the constitutional amendment on March 23, 2026, and the full text went online for the first time on November 11, on the external publicity website Naenara. A new Article 2 territorial clause states that the territory includes "the land bordering the People's Republic of China and the Russian Federation to the north, and the Republic of Korea to the south." South Korea is, for the first time, written into the constitution as a separate bordering state; the preamble and main text drop unification-related wording such as "the northern half" and "national reunification." The flag's aspect ratio changes from 1:2 to 1:1.65, the flag getting shorter — the first change since the state's founding in 1948.

A Notice 16 Months Late

The Shanghai investigation and handling task force issued its notice on November 13: in July 2023, the parents of a girl with a rare genetic disease took the initiative to contact Qiu Zilong, vice-president of the Songjiang Research Institute at Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, hoping for a custom gene-editing therapy; on January 2, 2025, the ethics committee of Xinhua Hospital agreed to a clinical study; on March 24, 2025, the child received the injection; on March 31, 2025, she died despite resuscitation efforts; on February 18, 2026, the related paper was published in Nature; on July 23, 2026, a joint investigation by Science and Retraction Watch exposed the case.

The notice establishes four points: the preclinical safety research had major information gaps — the safety report from the non-human primate experiments was not passed on to the clinical lead in time; the ethics committee's review was lax, approving the study while the evidence was insufficient; the paper concealed the animal-experiment safety report's findings and the child's death; and the family contributed 5.337 million yuan through donations, while team members privately accepted about 1.06 million yuan in gifts from the family. The disciplinary list runs to seven items covering eight people, of whom Qiu and Yang were dismissed.

Why It Happened

The Options Trade: Precise Structure, Tamer Suspense

The trade is called a "ratio put spread." Buying 180,000 puts struck at $150 is like buying an insurance policy: by January 15, 2027, if the share price falls below $150, the holder has the right to sell the shares to the counterparty at $150 and pocket the difference. Selling 280,000 puts struck at $100 is selling another "crash policy" to someone else and collecting the premium, using that income to offset most of the cost of the bought leg — which is how the net cost was squeezed to $8.32 million. Put simply, the bet is: Nvidia shares fall hard, but not below $100.

If the price closes exactly at $100 at expiry, the gross profit is 180,000 contracts × 100 shares × $50 = $900 million, and net of cost about $892 million — the theoretical maximum, which the Financial Times also states as "maximum profit of about $892 million."

The show got the downside risk wrong in two places. First, it said "if the share price crashes below $100, he could end up losing money." Checked leg by leg: at $90 the trade still makes about $792 million, at $50 about $392 million; it only turns into a loss below about $10.83, with a theoretical maximum loss of about $108 million if the price falls to zero. The Financial Times' correct formulation is "it could only lose if the price falls far below $100." Second, the show said "the most he can lose is the $8.32 million." That only holds if the price is above $150 at expiry. On the upside he loses $8.32 million; on the extreme downside he loses $108 million — that is the true shape of a ratio spread. With more puts sold than bought, the downside risk is capped only by the share price going to zero.

The size has to be written in full too: the bought leg covers 18 million shares, the sold leg 28 million, 46 million shares of notional in total. The show's "about 20 million shares involved" is roughly the bought leg alone and does not describe the whole position.

The show's most gripping question was "how did he know on October 12 that something was about to happen." First, correct the date: Jensen Huang admitted the affair on November 8 — in his staff letter he wrote "I was unfaithful to my wife… there is no excuse," without naming the other person; November 12 was the day he resigned. The show put the admission on November 12, four days late, and "exactly one month from October 12 to the admission" falls with it. Counted from November 8, the gap is 27 days.

Once the date is corrected, the suspense itself needs taming too. No Tier 1/2 source confirms that this trader knew about the scandal in advance or had any connection to the forces behind it. The Financial Times reported only the position itself and the maths, with no suggestion of insider trading, and the trader's identity is unknown. When the position was opened on October 12, Nvidia had already fallen from its October 6 record high of $239.24 to $226.32, and bearish voices on AI valuations and Nvidia's customer concentration were already out there. This trade could perfectly well be a bearish bet or hedge built on valuation and volatility, not an insider trade on a scandal.

The show also said "in the end he would definitely sell over the counter to a big investment bank, getting 10% to 20% less than $200 million." That figure has no source anywhere. The Financial Times original mentions no over-the-counter sale, no big bank, no discount. Dumping 460,000 contracts on-exchange in one go would certainly move the market — that is reasonable market common sense; but the specific route of "over the counter to a big bank" and the "10% to 20% discount" are both uncorroborated. In the finished piece this sentence can only be written as the show's speculation, not as fact.

Ukraine: The Price Is Named, Delivery Depends on Three Sides

The Abu Dhabi talks ran two days, November 10–11, and ended with no major breakthrough, agreement only on a prisoner exchange of more than 1,000 people. After the talks the US side floated the "Donbas for NATO" hypothetical. Zelensky's four conditions plus a referendum are the Ukrainian side's terms; the US side has not confirmed them, and "the specifics have not yet been determined" — Zelensky's own words. The US side has already answered clearly that it will not send US troops onto Ukrainian territory.

The Associated Press approached it from another angle the same day: Zelensky voiced concern about the new European security framework the US side proposed, believing Moscow could use such discussions to weaken NATO and redraw Europe's borders. The order he insists on is: ceasefire, an end to the war, security guarantees for Ukraine — and only then a discussion of the European security architecture.

The show's list of Putin's demands needs three corrections after fact-checking. First, "Finland and Sweden joining NATO delayed" is wrong. Finland joined NATO in April 2023 and Sweden in March 2024 — both are done deals. What actually appeared in reporting is new: Germany's Bild said a Russian informal proposal asked Finland to leave NATO, but the Kremlin denied it on November 13. Second, "NATO returning to its 1997 deployment lines" is what Putin aide Ushakov said on November 12, and an old demand from Russia's two draft treaties of December 2021 — not a new item on this round's negotiating list confirmed by the AP piece. Third, "controlling Eastern European countries' defence systems" is likewise closer to the old 2021 draft demands.

The show also said Witkoff and Kushner "flew straight to Finland after meeting Putin." That is wrong. After more than two hours with Putin in Moscow on November 12, the two flew that evening directly to Dublin to meet Zelensky. Multiple Tier 1/2 sources agree; Reuters' original reads "Zelensky met Witkoff and Kushner in Ireland on Thursday." The "to Finland" version comes from a single social-media post, confirmed by no mainstream reporting. Kushner's "major progress" was an assessment of the negotiation process as a whole and the prisoner-exchange result, not of the specific "Donbas for NATO" idea.

North Korea: The Amendment Is From March; November Only Published the Text

The show said "Kim Jong Un announced the constitutional amendment on November 11" — wrong on both the date and the actor. The amendment was passed by the first session of the 15th Supreme People's Assembly on March 23, 2026; the full text went up on North Korea's external publicity website Naenara on November 11, 2026, its first public release in nearly three years.

Two other claims from the show need correcting as well. "The constitution deleted the phrase 'South Korea is the first hostile state'" does not hold. That phrase was never written into the constitution, so there was nothing to delete. What actually happened is that Kim Jong Un proposed in his January 15, 2024 policy speech to the Supreme People's Assembly that it be written into the constitution, but the amendment passed in March 2026 did not adopt it. "Kim Jong Un announced last year (2025) that he was giving up reunification" is also a year off. The turn away from reunification came at the Workers' Party plenum at the end of December 2023 and in the January 15, 2024 Supreme People's Assembly speech — the show put it a year late.

The show also said "the five-pointed star in a circle on the national emblem moved from the left toward the centre" — wrong in two ways. The star inside a white circle is a feature of the national flag, not the emblem; and Article 166 of the amended constitution still says the star sits "in a white circle on the hoist side of the red band" — its position has not changed. There has been no reporting of any change to the national emblem.

The Gene-Editing Case: A Stamped Experiment, a Tragedy No One Stopped

All four conclusions in the notice check out. First, the preclinical safety research had major information gaps. Qiu's team did not pass the non-human primate safety report's findings to the clinical lead at Xinhua Hospital in time, and the warning signals in the report went unheeded. The report was completed in February 2025; according to Science, all four treated monkeys developed moderate-to-severe liver damage, one high-dose monkey developed kidney damage and a potentially fatal thrombotic microangiopathy — the same condition that killed the child. Second, the study went into humans despite insufficient safety evidence. The ethics committee's review was lax: although it asked for supplementary material, it approved the clinical study before that material was supplied. Third, the paper concealed negative results: at publication it did not disclose the animal-experiment safety report completed in February 2025 or the child's death; separately, the paper was found to have no data fabrication, but it did have methodological and descriptive inconsistencies and undisclosed conflicts of interest. Fourth, money and conflicts of interest: the family contributed 5.337 million yuan through donations (3.458 million paid to a Guangzhou company to prepare the viral vector, 1.379 million to a Sichuan company for large-animal toxicology studies, plus a 500,000-yuan donation to an education development foundation); team members privately accepted about 1.06 million yuan in gifts from the family. The show mentioned only 100,000 and 900,000 yuan, leaving out the 30,000 yuan each accepted by Zhang and Yuan, and the electronic device accepted by Qiu.

The show said "seven people disciplined, two of them dismissed" — the count is wrong. The notice's disciplinary list runs to seven items covering eight people: Qiu and Yang were both dismissed, barred from undertaking or participating in science and technology programmes, awards and honours for five years, and entered into the serious research-integrity misconduct database; Yu was removed as director of the research department and demoted two grades; Shi had his Party post revoked, was removed as director of the ethics office and demoted one grade; Sun had his Party post revoked and was demoted one grade; Xu received a serious Party warning; Zhang and Yuan received administrative warnings. On the institutional side, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine and Xinhua Hospital were summoned and ordered to submit written self-criticisms and rectify. The notice mentions no one being handed over to the judiciary; the question of criminal liability has no official answer yet; and the paper in question had still not been retracted when the notice was issued.

The show put the date of death at March 21 — ten days early. Treatment (March 24) and death (March 31) were seven days apart, and the sequence holds together; the show simply moved the death earlier. The child was diagnosed with Snijders Blok–Campeau syndrome (a rare childhood neurodevelopmental disorder caused by a CHD3 gene mutation), with only 237 known cases worldwide; she was six years old and her symptoms were relatively mild.

What Really Matters

Four stories, four versions of "the rules failed."

In the options market, the rules are options pricing and disclosure. Someone worked out $150 a month in advance — whether by information or by judgement, there is still no answer. The rules did not forbid him from placing the bet; they only require that, once placed, the result be tested by the market. A 24x return is not itself illegal; insider trading is, and to this day there is no evidence of it. That is the market's rule. It does not ask why you knew; it only asks whether you breached any disclosure duty when you bet.

At the Russia–Ukraine negotiating table, the rule is Article 10 of the North Atlantic Treaty: inviting a new member requires unanimous agreement, and a candidate can only join once all 32 member states have completed ratification. The US side can put NATO membership on the table as a bargaining chip, but a NATO invitation is not America's alone to give. Zelensky's four conditions plus a referendum hand the decision back to Ukrainian society — itself a form of resistance to the "deal." Territory is not a chip on the negotiators' table; it is something only a national referendum can decide.

On the Korean Peninsula, the rule is the constitution. Kim Jong Un used an amendment to write division into the constitution, closing the door to reunification in legal terms. But the constitution is a menu in his hands, changeable at any time. Today's "two states" written in can be written back out tomorrow. A dictator's moves have to be taken seriously — but not too seriously.

At Shanghai Jiao Tong, the rule is ethics review. The ethics committee stamped its approval, the experiment went into a human body anyway, and the child died anyway. The rules were there all along; the people who stamped them did not enforce them. The 2018 He Jiankui case was forged ethics-review documents and a privately organised procedure; this time the procedure was approved by an ethics committee, but the approval itself was lax. The latter is scarier. One had no genuine approval; the other had approval that failed in its duty.

My Analysis

The options trade is the story most worth unpacking in this issue. A 24x return is not luck; it is the precise structure of "bet on a big fall, but not below $100." Its cost structure uses "selling" to subsidise "buying" — $8.32 million levering 46 million shares of notional. The leverage comes from the structure, not from the capital. Its value before expiry is elastic: as the price fell from $226 to $166, only about 9% from $150, more people came to believe the price would reach $150, and the options became worth more. That is how paper gains are made.

The show asks "can this model be copied?" The answer splits in two: the structure can be copied, the timing cannot. A ratio spread is a standard Wall Street tool; anyone can build one the same way. But that date of October 12, that strike price, that expiry — those were the trader's own judgement. Treating 24x as a replicable formula is the biggest misreading of this trade.

Whether the Ukraine deal can work does not depend on what price Washington names, but on whether Zelensky's four conditions and the referendum can actually happen. Most likely they cannot. The four conditions themselves have "specifics not yet determined," and the referendum's outcome is even less controllable. Whether a country would vote to cede a fifth of its own territory — the answer is almost written into the question. Donbas's "fortress belt" is a fortification system the Ukrainian army has built up over more than a decade; Russian forces are currently advancing less than 100 square kilometres a month and hold about 81% of Donetsk Oblast. The military value for Russia of demanding the whole of Donetsk Oblast is removing that fortification system — not, as the show put it, "a land route through Zaporizhzhia to Crimea." That land corridor was already opened in May 2022.

The real weak point of the gene-editing case, that the ethics committee approved it while the university itself is absent from the liability list, is worth putting ahead of how many people were disciplined. The notice only summoned Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine and ordered rectification, with no personal discipline at the medical school's leadership level. The show's claim that "the authorities are avoiding the university's responsibility" and "playing it down" is the show's commentary and has to be labelled as such in the finished piece. But two factual footnotes are worth writing in: in September 2025, the Yangpu District Health Commission fined the hospital only about $3,600 for "failing to properly supervise," and Qiu received no public discipline at the time; and every handling basis listed in the notice is Party discipline, administrative discipline or violation-handling rules — criminal liability is not mentioned.

The show also extended the topic to anti-corruption, claiming "20% to 25% of Central Committee members have all become criminals." That sentence has no official or authoritative backing anywhere, and it is banned from the finished piece. The closest number is that attendance at the Fifth Plenary Session was about 25.8% lower than in 2022 — an attendance gap with many causes, not a crime rate. Reading an attendance gap as a crime rate is the one error in this episode that should never have appeared.

The Other Explanation

For the options trade, another explanation is that the trade looks more like a textbook volatility trade, and pinning "he knew about the scandal in advance" on it does not fit. When the position was opened on October 12, Nvidia had already fallen from its October 6 record high of $239.24 to $226.32, and bearish voices on AI valuations and Nvidia's customer concentration were already out there. Michael Burry disclosed a bearish position at the end of September. The trader may have been betting on a valuation pullback, not on a scandal. The scandal was only the catalyst that sped the pullback up, not the premise of the trade.

For Ukraine, another explanation is that the US side's "question" is itself a negotiating technique. Putting NATO membership on the table to test where Kyiv's bottom line sits may be its real purpose; actually granting it comes second. Zelensky's four conditions plus a referendum are likewise a negotiating technique: kick the ball back to the US side and NATO, and hand the decision to a national referendum, so that no side can accuse him of "selling territory." Both sides are using "questions" and "conditions" to probe each other; the real deal has not yet begun.

For the North Korean amendment, another explanation is that the real audience is the United States, not South Korea. Kim Jong Un's negotiating counterpart has always been the United States, not South Korea. Writing South Korea into the constitution as "another country" tells Washington: on Peninsula issues, Seoul has no say — if you want to talk, come to Pyongyang directly.

For the gene-editing case, another explanation is that this is a necessary product of "asymmetric rewards and responsibility" in China's research system, not an isolated ethics accident. Universities are mostly state-run: when research succeeds, the credit goes to the university, the state and the local government; when it fails, it is the individual's fault. With rights, responsibilities and rewards out of balance, an ethics committee will not stop a reckless experiment. The brand names of a top hospital and a famous university also make patients drop their guard. The child's parents, desperate to save their daughter and spending more than 5 million yuan on research, both trusted a big hospital and a well-known professor — and may have been talked up by the rosy prospects the professor painted. Nobody doubted. Nobody questioned.

What It Means for Business and the Economy

For investors, the options trade means the market always has someone placing bets where you cannot see them, and the bets are bigger than you imagine. 460,000 contracts, 46 million shares of notional — this is not a retail gamble, it is an institutional-sized position. Nvidia has fallen from $239 to $162, more than 30% — how much of that is the scandal, how much is a valuation pullback, how much is the broader market, cannot be separated. But one thing is certain. November 17 brings Nvidia's earnings, November 18 a closed-door congressional briefing — the next set of hard markers. Whether the price keeps moving toward $150 will decide whether the paper gains in this options position turn into real money.

For the European security order, the Ukraine deal means territory has, for the first time, been openly priced as a NATO ticket. If this precedent is set, NATO changes its nature: from an organisation that defends its members' territory to one that can help another country cede territory. NATO has still made no invitation to Ukraine, and as recently as February 2025 the US defence secretary publicly called Ukrainian membership "unrealistic." Even if the US idea is genuine, it is only a negotiating notion, not a NATO commitment.

For East Asia, the North Korean amendment means the Peninsula's division has a constitutional text for the first time. South Korean President Lee Jae-myung asked China in January 2026 to act as a "mediator" on Peninsula issues — that was in January, and has no direct connection to this November publication of the amendment. But after the amendment, Washington and Seoul will have to rethink the scale of and investment in US Forces Korea. Rubio has said both that Kim Jong Un is not expanding and, following Trump's cost-cutting logic, that weakening US Forces Korea would then have a pretext. The show judges that the Trump administration will find it hard to change the status quo of US military investment in South Korea in any obvious way, but the risk exists in the rhetoric.

For Chinese research, the gene-editing case means that if an ethics review stamp does not equal gatekeeping, the stamp itself becomes a risk amplifier. The patient's family spent more than 5 million yuan on a paper, purely for the sake of the paper. The brand names of a top hospital and a famous university made the patients drop the vigilance they needed. And the investigation came from the leadership organs of the hospital and the university — investigating themselves, how can it be open, fair and just? The findings will inevitably protect the reputations of Shanghai Jiao Tong and Xinhua Hospital and push the blame onto a few "bad apples." Similar violations will keep happening unless oversight steps outside the system.

What to Watch Next

  • Whether Nvidia's share price keeps moving toward $150, and whether the mystery trader closes the position before expiry to lock in the gain; Nvidia earnings on November 17, the closed-door congressional briefing on November 18.
  • The next round of US–Russia–Ukraine talks: whether Zelensky's four conditions and the referendum idea develop further, and whether the US side publicly confirms the "Donbas for NATO" proposal.
  • US and South Korean reactions after the North Korean amendment, and any signal of a change in the scale of US Forces Korea.
  • The full disciplinary list for the Shanghai Jiao Tong case, whether the paper is retracted, and whether anyone is handed over to the judiciary.

A Question to Close

The rules are written on paper — who guarantees they are enforced? The options market relies on market testing, NATO on unanimity, the constitution on whoever writes it, and ethics review on whoever stamps it. Of these four answers, which one worries you most?
