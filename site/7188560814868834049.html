Nov 13 Headlines: Jensen Huang Resigns, Guo Shuqing Floats an Inheritance Tax, Vance Gets Named — Where the Rules Can't Reach, the Bill Gets Settled Another Way

November 13, 2026 · Read the original (Chinese)

Xiao Wang's Daily Finance · November 13, 2026 · Content period: November 12 · ~11 min read

Today's lineup: Jensen Huang resigns · $7.5 billion in chips · HK01's registry check adds a link · Guo Shuqing on inheritance tax · Vance and the Venezuela oil licences

Hook: A Resignation Is Not a Full Stop

On November 12, Nvidia announced that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive officer and director, effective immediately. Chief financial officer Colette Kress becomes interim CEO while keeping her CFO role, the board has begun a search for a successor, and longtime director Harvey C. Jones becomes the company's first chairman.

Read on its own, this looks like the end of a personal scandal. But on the same day, two other things were happening: an essay by Guo Shuqing on pensions and inheritance tax set Chinese social media alight, and Reuters reported a Venezuela oil thread connecting Vice President Vance with Vitol trader John Addison — while Trump publicly told Vance to turn his full attention back to crime and corruption at home.

The three stories involve different people, countries and fields, but they share one structure: a problem the formal rules were supposed to handle was never resolved through formal procedure, so the parties switched to naming names, leaks and legislative trial balloons to settle the bill. This edition lays out the verified facts first, then says whether that reading holds up.

First, the evidence grade. Huang's resignation itself and the Kress and Jones appointments come from Nvidia's company announcement and its SEC 8-K — the hardest layer. The Axios, The Information and Reuters reports all cite anonymous sources; they are reported accounts, not official documents. The HK01 registry report writes only what the registry search has confirmed, Guo Shuqing's essay has no full text available, and any claim not found in the published summaries is either qualified or left out.

What Happened: Three Threads Converge on the Same Day

First: Jensen Huang Resigns, Nvidia Enters Its Fourth Stage

Nvidia's November 12 announcement gives no reason for the resignation. That sentence has to come first: neither the company announcement nor the 8-K states a reason or mentions an extramarital affair. What can be written is the sequence in time — the resignation came after two extramarital affairs were exposed and after reports that the board was preparing to remove him.

The second affair was reported by The New York Times on November 11, involving Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare, on the accounts of five current employees. The first involved Huang Le. The night before, The Wall Street Journal reported that the board was preparing to remove Huang.

The succession is clear. Kress, 59, serves as interim CEO and retains her role as executive vice president and CFO; the board has begun a formal search for a successor, considering internal and external candidates; Jones, 73, a director since 1993 and a former Synopsys CEO and executive chairman, has been appointed the board's first chairman.

The shareholder picture must be written in the verified order. By several 13F compilations, the largest shareholder is Vanguard, at about 8%–9%; BlackRock is about 8%, second; State Street about 4%, third; Fidelity about 4%, fourth. Huang's latest holding is about 3.4% (about 3.37% on the September 17 Form 4), against an earlier June reading of 3.6%. Institutional holdings move with quarterly 13F filings, and the figures depend on the counting method.

The Information's new reporting pushes the affair from scandal toward export controls. Its November 9 report said Huang Le used her relationship with Jensen Huang to help move at least $7.5 billion — about 240,000 restricted Nvidia chips — to Chinese companies through Megaspeed and its Malaysian subsidiary Speedmatrix. The earlier public figure was that Megaspeed had bought at least $4.8 billion in advanced AI equipment since June 2024; $7.5 billion is roughly double that scale. All of it is a media allegation, Megaspeed denies wrongdoing, and nobody has been charged.

A November 12 report added another layer: entities linked to Huang Le took part, through special-purpose vehicles, in DeepSeek's second funding round (July–October, raising ¥105.5 billion, about $15.8 billion); DeepSeek staff pitched Shanghai Century Litong and its parent, Jiangsu Lettall Electronics (together, Litong Inc.), to prospective investors as a key partner able to supply a stable flow of restricted Blackwell chips. One correction must be written in full: the report also states clearly that Huang Le holds no direct stake in Litong Inc. — the link runs through her sister Huang Yu, her husband Kang Wei, and a chain of ownerships and company transactions. Nvidia's most advanced AI chip architecture is called Blackwell; the show mispronounced the chip's name, and this edition writes Blackwell throughout.

The smuggling route is written only where sources exist. According to reports, the chips moved through Singapore and Malaysia: Speedmatrix received more than 200 shipments of restricted Nvidia technology between June 2024 and June 2025, with the chips ending up in Shanghai Century Litong data centres in China. Megaspeed has a subsidiary in Indonesia, and customs data shows it importing H100 hardware, but there is no direct reporting to support "smuggled via Indonesia into China," so this edition does not state it as fact.

HK01's registry report supplies another chain of evidence, but only the registry-confirmed parts are used. A Hong Kong Economic Journal registry search confirms that Huang Le has been a director and sole shareholder of the Hong Kong company "VFO International Group Ltd." since January 2024; according to a customs-data platform, VFO has 121 transactions with a single supplier, PT Megaspeed International Indonesia (Megaspeed's Indonesian subsidiary); in early August 2025, she transferred all of her VFO shares to the BVI company PINE VALE (HK) LIMITED. The founding year and renaming dates have no corroboration and are left out. Century Litong obtained Nvidia preferred cloud partner status in 2023 — that is sourced and can be written.

On Huang Le's relationship with China's Ministry of State Security, this edition writes only the verified version: according to The New York Times, US intelligence agencies concluded through surveillance that Huang Le had been recruited by the ministry to gather intelligence on Jensen Huang, and discovered her relationship with him in the process. Most public reporting says "intelligence agencies" — no report says the FBI carried out the surveillance; Trump has only confirmed that the FBI is "investigating" the matter. Claims that it went on "for a long time," was discovered "by accident," or about how much of the calls' contents were known have no public reporting behind them and are not used.

Ming-Chi Kuo posted on X on November 12 with a cooler market judgment. He called the resignation a "one-off clearing event" for the stock: once Huang has fully left, the direct impact of congressional summonses and investigations on the share price will shrink. The share-price figures: over the two trading days of November 9–10, NVDA fell from a November 6 close of $186.64 to $168.89 (−9.5%); after the Journal's removal report came out after hours on November 11, it fell about another 8.6% to about $159; on November 12 it closed at $166.19, trading around $165 during the day.

Kuo also said Google's TPU is the biggest beneficiary, with MediaTek and Broadcom the key partners — Broadcom is an American company, headquartered in California; the succession impact will show earliest in 2028; capacity bookings and specification upgrades are the key supply-chain focus. China demand and export controls are the key variables for the share price, but he gave no specific figure for the share of chips flowing into China, so this edition adds none.

Congress reacted across party lines, but the focus has moved past Huang himself. Republican Senator Kevin Cramer told Politico on November 11 that Huang's involvement posed a national-security risk — "he has to go" — and backed issuing a subpoena if necessary; he had earlier called on Huang to step aside temporarily. Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren said on November 12 that the resignation "does not absolve him," and that Huang and other Nvidia executives "still owe Congress an accounting" on export-control compliance; she and Chris Coons, about to chair the Senate Foreign Relations Committee, wrote to Lutnick, Rubio and White House chief of staff Miller demanding the preservation of all documents related to Nvidia's chip exports to China. Three House committees (Foreign Affairs, the Select Committee on China, and Intelligence) have asked for a closed-door briefing on November 18; no subpoena has been issued yet.

The White House thread tightened the same day. Axios reported on November 11–12, citing six senior sources, that Trump approved action against companies suspected of diverting Nvidia chips in May, August and again on November 5, and that Lutnick stalled each time; at the November 5 Situation Room meeting, Treasury Secretary Bessent asked, "Why isn't Megaspeed on the entity list yet?" and Lutnick replied, "I don't know, I'll look into it." This edition must also carry the Commerce Department's denial: a department spokesperson denied that Bessent and Lutnick ever had that conversation. That the entity list has had no additions in over a year is confirmed by multiple sources.

Bloomberg reported on November 12 that, hours after Huang's resignation, White House officials said "President Trump has complete confidence in Secretary Lutnick and believes he is up to the job, as one of the most transformational Commerce Secretaries in modern history." The same day, Reuters separately reported that senior White House officials were already discussing whether to push Lutnick out. Both signals exist side by side; this edition carries both and takes neither side.

Beijing also spoke on November 12. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said at the regular press briefing that China's technological progress rests on independent innovation and open cooperation, and that the US should stop fabricating so-called "China spy stories" as a pretext for technology restrictions. Mao Ning also said she was "not aware" of whether Huang Le is in Beijing; on November 10, spokesperson Guo Jiakun had already called the allegations "pure fabrication, intended to smear China." The APEC summit in Shenzhen runs November 18–19, with a third Trump–Xi meeting expected; the Busan truce was due to expire on November 10, 2026, was extended after the September Washington summit to January 10, 2027, and the APEC window is the key moment for renewing it.

Second: Guo Shuqing's Essay — the Pension Gap Forces the Inheritance-Tax Question

Guo Shuqing, former chairman of the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission, former governor of Shandong and former president of China Construction Bank, now serves as vice-chairman of the Financial and Economic Affairs Committee of the 14th National People's Congress. An essay of his was published on November 11 in Social Security Review, on the sustainability of pensions and health insurance, and quickly set Chinese social media alight after publication.

First, a hard error in the show to correct: the essay was not published this September — the show's publication date, journal name and timeline of circulation were all wrong. Several November 11–12 summaries agree that November 11 was the first publication, and the concentrated November coverage is the normal spread after a first publication.

As publicly summarised, the essay makes three points. First, start legislation on inheritance and gift taxes promptly, and study using the new revenue to top up pensions. Second, transfer state capital to shore up social security: the full summary says China's state-owned enterprises hold net assets of about ¥140 trillion, and proposes continuing to transfer state capital into the social security fund. Third, health-insurance funds hold accumulated balances of nearly ¥6 trillion, so improve the efficiency of fund investment and operations, and study using some ultra-long special treasury bonds and local special-purpose bond funds to subsidise urban and rural residents' pensions.

Two further points need qualification. The summaries confirm Guo wrote that "an entrepreneur not taking a salary and not paying individual income tax is not the point — the point is that family living expenses must not be charged to the company"; but the specific wording about "adjusting the fiscal spending structure, cutting investment in production-oriented projects and raising spending on public services and pension security" does not appear in the published summaries, the full text has not been obtained, and this edition writes it as "according to the essay's argument." The essay is a policy proposal, not a legislative decision; China currently has no timetable for inheritance or gift tax legislation.

Why did this essay explode? Because it laid the pension ledger bare. The Chinese Academy of Social Sciences' China Pension Actuarial Report 2019–2050 projects that the accumulated balance of the basic pension fund for urban enterprise employees will run out by 2035. The report's lead, Zheng Bingwen, later clarified that a depleted balance does not mean young people will get no pension — current pensions are paid mainly from current contributions by young workers, with the gap covered by fiscal and other resources. Reading a depleted balance as the end of the pension system is a misreading, and this edition does not use it.

The other end of public anger is house prices. The show presented local extremes as the national picture. The verified version: S&P estimated in October 2026 that Chinese residential prices have fallen about 22% from their 2021 peak; the BIS real house-price index is down about 24.7% from its peak; drops of more than 40% appear only in some third- and fourth-tier cities. This edition writes "the national average is down about one-fifth from its 2021 peak, with falls of over 40% in some third- and fourth-tier cities."

The show's US comparison uses figures that are themselves correct and can stay. As amended by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 4, 2025), the 2026 US federal estate-tax basic exclusion is $15 million per person, $30 million per couple through portability, with a top rate of 40% above that. As an example: parents leaving a $1 million home, $2 million in stocks, $500,000 in cash and $500,000 in other assets — $4 million in total — are far below the $15 million single-person exclusion, so the federal estate tax is zero. One qualification must be added: some states levy their own estate or inheritance taxes, for example Oregon, which starts at $1 million.

The essay mentions no exemption threshold, and the authorities have no threshold proposal. Guo Shuqing's essay only proposes starting legislative study; what the threshold would be and who it would cover are all undecided.

Third: Two Vance Stories — Iran and Venezuela

Reuters reported on November 12 that Trump wrote on social media that day, "We don't need a deal with Iran… We got their money, hundreds of billions of dollars, and their strait," and instructed Vance to put his "entire focus" on crime and corruption in Democratic-run cities and states, saying that correcting large-scale criminal activity could quickly balance the budget.

The day before, The Wall Street Journal reported exclusively that Vance was considering a face-to-face meeting with Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council (a hardliner and former Revolutionary Guards commander); Trump had authorised Vance to lead a restart of negotiations; Vance wrote to Rezaei in October requesting a meeting; the talks remain at an early stage, with no date or venue set, and Doha has been discussed as a possible location. Rezaei has publicly denied that negotiations are under way, calling the reports "pure lies"; Reuters has not independently confirmed a potential meeting. This edition must carry the denial alongside.

Another Reuters exclusive, published November 11, concerns Venezuela oil. In the week before the US invaded Venezuela in January and seized Maduro, flight records show Addison's private Embraer jet and Air Force Two both on the tarmac at Bozeman Yellowstone International Airport on December 27. One source (relaying what Addison told him) said the two met at the Yellowstone Club and discussed Venezuela, with Addison assuring Vance that Vitol could quickly sell Venezuelan oil.

This passage must carry the denials and the sourcing level in full: Addison himself and Vance's office both denied to Reuters that any meeting took place on December 27; the meeting claim rests on a single source's second-hand account, and the flight records only prove both aircraft were at the same airport on the same day — they cannot prove the two men met. This edition must not write the meeting as an established fact.

The chain after that is undisputed. Addison himself confirmed to Reuters that on January 5 (two days after Maduro was toppled), Energy Secretary Chris Wright called him from his Houston office to ask whether Vitol would be willing to sell Venezuelan oil; the Energy Department confirmed the call. Before January 9, both Vitol and Trafigura had received special licences for Venezuelan oil; the two companies have since earned hundreds of millions of dollars selling Venezuelan crude. Reuters confirmed that the government skipped competitive bidding, on the grounds that rapid action was needed to stabilise Venezuela's economy after Maduro's capture; the Energy Department also acknowledged Vitol was invited directly. The department says Vance and Wright never discussed Venezuelan oil sales, and that Vitol was chosen because it is the world's largest energy trader.

Addison's background must also be written in the corrected version. FEC records show he donated $300,000 in 2022 to Protect Ohio Values PAC, supporting Vance's Ohio Senate campaign. In 2024, he and his wife Shannon gave a combined $7 million to several political action committees, $5 million of it to MAGA Inc. — not $7 million from him personally "to the Trump campaign." In 2024, Vitol and SK Energy settled with California for $50 million after a decade of litigation over alleged manipulation of California gasoline prices; Addison was the most senior Vitol executive named in the case — it was not a settlement he personally reached with California. Addison retired from Vitol in March 2026 and now chairs Wright's energy policy advisory committee.

Why It Happened: Where the Formal Procedures Jammed

The three threads jammed at different points, but the way they jammed looks alike.

On the Nvidia thread, the jam is at the enforcement layer. The entity list has had no additions in over a year; the Axios timeline shows Trump approving controls three times and Lutnick stalling three times, with Bessent forced to ask the question out loud in the Situation Room. The Commerce Department's denial keeps the matter at the level of "reported accounts," but the fact that "this company is not on the list" is confirmed by multiple sources. The rule was not overturned — it simply was not enforced.

On the Guo Shuqing thread, the jam is over where the money comes from. The pension fund's accumulated balance is projected, on the 2019 actuarial forecast, to run out by 2035, and the model of current contributions plus fiscal subsidies is getting tighter; the essay's prescription is to transfer state capital, raise the efficiency of fund operations, and study using new tax revenue to top up pensions. Inheritance and gift taxes are sensitive because they put "taxing the stock of wealth" on the table for the first time — and the stock of wealth is mostly housing, with the national average already down about one-fifth from its peak, and household assets having just shrunk.

On the Vance thread, the jam is in the procedure itself. The Venezuelan oil sales licences skipped competitive bidding; the Energy Department admits Vitol was invited directly, on the grounds of time pressure. Set against the chain — the phone call first, then the licences, then retirement followed by a seat on the energy policy advisory committee — that reason is hard to read cleanly on its own. Reuters reached no judicial conclusion; it laid out a timeline and a set of interests, and left the judgment to readers and to Congress.

What Really Matters: The Way the Bill Gets Settled Has Changed

The truly important change in this edition is not any one scandal, but the way the bill gets settled.

In the past, problems like these were supposed to be handled by rules. Export controls were for the Commerce Department's list, pension shortfalls for fiscal and social-security adjustments, government contracts for competitive bidding. In this edition, none of the three rulebooks worked normally — the list was not updated, the gap was not filled, the bidding never happened. So the parties switched to means outside the rules: media scoops, a board acting, letters from Congress demanding documents be preserved, a Treasury secretary questioning a colleague in public in the Situation Room, a policy essay setting off a nationwide debate on social media.

These means share one feature: they do not depend on a procedure's conclusion, only on public pressure. Huang's resignation was not the result of a board vote to remove him, but the result of reporting, scoops and market pressure piling up; Guo Shuqing's essay is not a legislative decision, but the day after publication it became a tax question debated by the whole country; Reuters's reporting on Vance reached no judicial conclusion, but it put the timeline, the donation figures and the licence chain on the table and forced each party to respond one by one. Taken together, the three stories show that when the formal rules cannot stop money and power, naming names, scoops and legislative trial balloons become the actual instruments of reckoning.

My Analysis: A Resignation Is Not the End — the Investigation Has Just Shifted Gear

First, Huang's resignation does not end the affair. Nvidia's November 17 earnings and the November 18 congressional closed-door briefing are the next pair of hard dates. Earnings are Kress's debut; the briefing is Congress's first formal demand for an explanation, and Warren and Coons's demand that documents be preserved shows the investigation already reaches beyond the company. Whether the investigation burns toward Lutnick and the White House is a more important question than "who becomes CEO." The White House gave two signals on the same day — full backing for Lutnick, and discussions about pushing him out — which shows the White House itself has not decided.

Second, Kuo's judgment deserves to be taken seriously, but analysis and fact must be kept apart. His claims that the resignation is a "one-off clearing event," that the succession impact will show earliest in 2028, and that capacity bookings and specification upgrades are the supply-chain focus are analysis, and can be debated. His point that China demand and export controls are the key variables for the share price is supported by fact. But he gave no specific figure for the share of chips flowing into China, and two other claims added in the show have no source and are not used here. What investors really need to watch is the China revenue and Blackwell shipment figures in the November 17 earnings, and whether the successor can deliver on the capacity expectations Huang left behind.

Third, the substance of Guo Shuqing's essay is a pension gap forcing a search for new tax revenue, but its political meaning is bigger than its fiscal meaning. That a policy essay could set the whole internet alight the day after publication shows public sensitivity to "taxing the stock of wealth" has already reached a tipping point. The essay mentions no threshold and the authorities have no proposal, but the debate has already started ahead of any proposal. That means the coming legislative debate will no longer be a technical one, but a distributional one — whose houses, whose inheritances, who fills the gap.

Fourth, Vance's troubles are only beginning, but this edition must stay restrained. Reuters's reporting carries denials from both parties up front, and the meeting claim rests on a single source, so it cannot be written as settled. What does stand is the undisputed chain: the January 5 call, the licences before January 9, hundreds of millions of dollars in sales revenue, the skipped bidding process, and the advisory-committee seat after the March retirement. That chain alone is enough for Congress to pursue, without adding an unconfirmed meeting to weigh it down.

The Other Explanation: Could All of This Be Overstated?

In fairness, the other side deserves a hearing.

First, the Journal's board report, the Times' second affair, the Axios White House infighting report and the Reuters Venezuela exclusive all rely on anonymous sources. A board meeting is not a removal, Bessent's question may be a public airing of a long-running tension between the Treasury and Commerce secretaries, and the Reuters meeting claim rests on a single second-hand source. Treating reported accounts as fact is the easiest mistake to make in this edition.

Second, Kuo's "one-off clearing" may be too optimistic. What the resignation clears is personnel uncertainty, not the investigation itself. The November 18 closed-door briefing, the Justice Department's criminal investigation, and the scope of Congress's document-preservation demand all show the investigation has just shifted gear. The share price closing at $166.19 on November 12 does not mean the market has priced in all the risk.

Third, Guo Shuqing's essay may not carry that much policy weight. It is a policy proposal published in an academic journal, not a legislative decision, and it has no timetable. Reading it as "the inheritance tax is coming right away" mistakes a proposal for a decision.

These counterarguments partly hold. But they cannot change three things: Huang has already resigned; Guo Shuqing's essay has already been published and the inheritance-tax question has already entered public debate; and the Vitol and Trafigura licences, the phone call and the revenue figures are already on the table, with each party already responding one by one. The other side can question the speed and the scale, but not the direction.

What It Means for Business and the Economy

For Nvidia investors: the near term is the November 17 earnings and the November 18 congressional briefing. Kuo says the succession impact will show earliest in 2028 and that revenue and profit over the next two years will be largely unaffected — that holds only if the successor can deliver on the capacity bookings and specification upgrades Huang left behind. Watch what the supply chain says, not the next chapter of the affair.

For the AI sector: Google's TPU is the clearest beneficiary of this round, with MediaTek and Broadcom named by Kuo as key partners. But the benefit holds only if the investigation does not spread: if the export-control loopholes are systematically tightened, every AI hardware company that depends on China demand will have to redo its sums.

For Chinese households: inheritance and gift tax legislation is currently only a proposal, with no timetable and no threshold plan. But the debate has already happened, and against a backdrop of house prices down about one-fifth from their peak, any hint of taxing the stock of housing wealth will be amplified. Whether the authorities say anything about thresholds and coverage next — silence itself is a signal.

For US politics and energy markets: the Venezuela oil licence chain shows that the way large government contracts are handed out is itself becoming a political issue. The stated reason for skipping the bidding was time pressure, but "who was invited directly" and "who earned hundreds of millions of dollars" are two questions that can be asked in public. The Energy Department says Vitol was chosen because it is the world's largest energy trader — a claim that does not contradict "the vice president's friend got the phone call first," and Congress will be asking precisely about the relationship between the two.

  • November 18 (Wednesday): the congressional closed-door briefing — whether Huang attends, whether a subpoena is issued; the APEC Shenzhen summit opens the same day, and whether the Xi–Trump meeting is affected by the Nvidia affair.
  • November 18–19: the APEC Shenzhen summit, the key window for renewing the truce — the deal was extended to January 10, 2027.

  • What follows Guo Shuqing's essay: whether the authorities give a timetable or threshold for inheritance and gift tax legislation — silence itself is a signal.
  • Congressional follow-up on the Vance–Addison affair: whether the meeting claim gets a second source, and whether the licence chain enters a formal investigation.

A Question to Close

One question to leave with you. When the formal rules can no longer be enforced, what replaces them is usually cruder pressure — a boardroom decision, a media scoop, a letter from Congress, a policy essay setting off a nationwide debate.
