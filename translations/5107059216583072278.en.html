A "flying" Roadster demo steered market attention toward a Tesla–SpaceX merger; Washington hit pause on Russia sanctions two days before they took effect; at home, the Zunjie V800 brake-pedal case escalated into a three-agency joint investigation.

1. After the Roadster "flew", markets bet on a Tesla–SpaceX merger

Per Bloomberg, US media and prediction-market data; event details based on media accounts, pending official confirmation

Austin — Tesla unveiled the next-generation Roadster in Texas on the evening of the 15th, staging the demo at a SpaceX rocket-test site. The car reportedly carries cold-gas thrusters co-developed with SpaceX, and the demo showed a brief liftoff and mid-air turns; Tesla claims 0–60 mph in under one second, and also demonstrated the car driving upside down using an adhesion system, with mass production targeted for 2028. Earlier reports had said the thrusters were extremely loud, the demo car would be remote-controlled, and spectators would have to stand hundreds of yards away.

The demo carried a second meaning for capital markets. Per data cited by the program, the Kalshi prediction market put the odds of the two companies merging at 56% after the event, up from 29%. Note that another report on October 15 put a similar contract at 19% — the two contracts' windows and methodologies may differ, so cite accordingly.

Reading it: it's the AI, not the car, driving expectations

Barclays analysts including Dan Levy said in an October 15 report that a merger could be announced before Tesla's annual shareholder meeting. Tesla's meeting is due no later than December 6, so an announcement is expected between late October and early November — the later the announcement, the harder it would be to win approval at the meeting. (Some media misread this as "merger to be announced on December 6" — December 6 is the latest date for the meeting itself.)

The report argues the merger's real value lies in combining the two companies' AI technology for vertical integration. Their ties across AI, chips and energy storage are already deep: xAI is reportedly a major customer for Tesla's large-scale storage batteries, the two are jointly advancing the Terafab chip-manufacturing project, and SpaceX has bought large numbers of Tesla electric trucks; the program cited the report as saying SpaceX bought roughly $300 million of storage equipment from Tesla in this year's second quarter. SpaceX acquired Musk's AI company xAI earlier this year, and since then the market's "next up: Tesla" expectations have kept building.

The deal structure remains undetermined; the program laid out three possibilities: SpaceX buys Tesla, which could dilute shareholders; a share swap with both remaining independent, but the valuation and governance gaps are wide; or keeping the status quo with deeper cooperation, though coordination would be less efficient. Barclays flags the biggest obstacle as the shareholder vote: at Tesla's 2025 meeting, about 11% of voting power counted as "broker non-votes," effectively equivalent to no votes. Who benefits more from a merger would also shape how both sets of shareholders feel.

Two windows to watch: Tesla's third-quarter earnings on October 21, mentioned by the program, and the shareholder meeting due by December 6. One reminder: Wall Street's enthusiasm for the merger and the flying sports car are two different things — the former rests on business-integration logic, the latter looks more like a public showcase.

2. Trump waives Graham Act sanctions on Russia; Zelenskyy condemns

Per Reuters, ABC, The Washington Post and The Wall Street Journal

Washington — President Trump signed an executive order on Friday the 16th waiving a package of sanctions and tariffs on Russia under the Graham Act, which had been due to take effect on October 18. Named after the late Republican senator Graham, who died in July this year, the act was signed into law by Trump on September 18. In the order, Trump submitted certification to Congress that the waiver serves US national interests, without explaining the specific reasons; the waiver will last until the administration determines "further action is necessary." ABC also reported that he waived a provision allowing sanctions to be lifted once Russia signs a peace deal accepted by Ukraine.

Under the act's design, tariffs of up to 100% would apply to goods from major importers of Russian oil and gas, as well as countries helping evade sanctions. Reuters notes the law lets the president waive sanctions by simply notifying Congress that they are not in the national interest — so the waiver has a legal basis, and the dispute is mostly political.

Reactions split quickly: Putin's investment envoy Kirill Dmitriev called it an "important and truly historic moment" on X; Democratic lawmakers including Bill criticized Trump for conceding to Putin, with several Republicans also voicing opposition; Zelenskyy condemned the decision on the 17th.

Reading it: oil prices, elections and peace talks tangled together

Reuters reports US–Ukraine ties hit a low this week: from threats to cut intelligence support for Ukraine, to a diesel deal that could help Russia raise war funds, to this waiver. Trump has also publicly criticized Zelenskyy and hinted Kyiv needs new leadership. Outside observers trace several intertwined threads:

Fox Business reported that Trump also announced an energy-sector ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine the same day, and said a diesel deal with Putin was done. Media including Reuters noted these moves objectively hand Russia immediate economic and diplomatic gains while Ukraine faces greater pressure. What to watch next: whether Congress moves to amend the law to limit waiver power; whether US pump prices and inflation data move noticeably; and whether Putin actually concedes anything on a ceasefire as a result. If the answer is no, Washington could simultaneously lose its economic leverage over Russia and erode Ukraine's trust in US security commitments.

3. US engages Myanmar's junta: scam compounds and rare earths in focus

Per Reuters

Washington / Tokyo — The US government has opened direct engagement with the military-led government of Myanmar, reversing the policy of isolating the junta after the 2021 coup, Reuters reports. Michael Vance, assistant secretary of state for intelligence and research, is playing a leading role. An October 8 report said the outreach initially focused on shutting down scam compounds targeting Americans, but could expand to Myanmar's heavy rare-earth deposits. Reuters now reports the two sides met for a second time in a month, in Tokyo last week, with more than 10 US officials taking part.

US officials acknowledged major obstacles, the reports say: rare-earth mines sit mostly in areas controlled by ethnic armed groups fighting the junta, and China is the main buyer of Myanmar's rare earths and may resist US involvement. The US also lifted sanctions last week on the daughter of a businessman accused of supporting Myanmar's military, describing it as part of the new Myanmar approach; the junta's leader remains under US sanctions.

Reading it: transactional, not reconciliation

The talks cover both scam compounds and rare earths — two different topics, but both circle back to the China factor: many scam compounds sit in border areas controlled by armed groups, as do the rare-earth producing regions. The move serves two US aims: finding rare-earth sources beyond China and re-establishing influence in Myanmar. Two high-level meetings in quick succession show Washington wants a substantive deal fast; but it does not mean a normalization of US–junta relations. For the junta, bringing in the US also diversifies its external dependencies. How this process affects China's rare-earth business and political influence in Myanmar is worth watching.

4. 17-year-old Kimi researcher Chen Guangyu departs, says he's headed to the US Bay Area

Per Lianhe Zaobao, Xinhua Finance, IT Home and DoNews

Shenzhen — Chen Guangyu, born in 2009, announced on social media on October 15 that he has left Moonshot AI, calling his year at Kimi the best of his life. He joined Moonshot AI in November 2025 as a machine learning researcher, was a co-first author of the March paper "Attention Residuals" and contributed to July's Kimi K3 release. The paper was publicly praised by Musk.

Under his post, Anthropic researcher Sholto Douglas invited him to join the Anthropic team in San Francisco; Chen thanked him and replied that he is on his way to the Bay Area. Lianhe Zaobao says he gave no definitive answer on whether he would join Anthropic. DoNews reported his LinkedIn profile showed he attended Carnegie Mellon University from August to September this year; a Silicon Valley investor had earlier mentioned a 17-year-old talent receiving multiple high-paying offers — widely speculated to be Chen, though he has not confirmed it himself.

Reading it: an unusual talent-flow sample

Chen has no complete university education, nor is he a conventional Chinese student abroad — he went to the US directly from core R&D at a top Chinese AI team, a path markedly different from past talent flows. Speculation about his destination abounds; his own disclosure will be the final word. More worth watching: cross-border movement of core model researchers is becoming a new variable in China–US AI competition.

5. Zunjie V800 brake-pedal bracket case escalates to joint investigation

Per CCTV, Beijing News, The Paper, MyDrivers and Central News Agency

Beijing — In response to online reports of "Zunjie V800 brake-pedal bracket fractures," the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, the Cyberspace Administration of China and the State Administration for Market Regulation announced on October 15 that a joint investigation team has been set up on the basis of earlier inquiries to verify the matter and handle it according to law, with updates to be released promptly. Zunjie Auto then issued a statement saying it would actively respond, fully obey and cooperate fully with the investigation team.

The incident began with a test video posted October 8 by the auto platform Dongchedi: three brand-new Zunjie V800s suffered pedal-bracket fractures during 100 km/h emergency-braking tests, breaking on the 2nd to 4th full-force braking attempts, with highly consistent positions and fracture patterns. The video showed that after the regular foot brake failed, only the P-gear emergency brake could stop the car, with braking distance stretching from about 38 meters to about 140 meters. The Zunjie V800, launched this August, is a million-yuan-class MPV co-built by Huawei's HarmonyOS Intelligent Mobility and JAC Motors, priced around ¥766,000–1,016,000; JAC's shares hit the daily limit down on the day of the incident.

Zunjie said that night the test was a "non-standard extreme test condition," that no such failure had occurred in real-world use since the first deliveries, and that it would optimize the design and offer free upgrades to delivered owners. On October 14, Zunjie released third-party inspection results from October 11, saying the component meets industry standard QC/T 788-2018, with displacement under 5 mm under the standard's 1,500 N longitudinal force. Note the inspection targeted the component itself, while Dongchedi's test involved repeated full-force braking — the two conditions cannot be directly compared.

Public focus: who is investigated, and how

Per the program, the Economic Daily published a commentary calling Dongchedi's business model one in which it is "both referee and player," making credibility hard to build, while stressing that building an auto powerhouse is at a critical stage and the big picture should come first; Huawei executive director Richard Yu also posted on social media that "justice may be late, but it will never be absent."

Public debate centers on three points. First, the investigation team includes the Cyberspace Administration — some question why a cyberspace agency is involved in a technical quality issue; others argue that scrutiny of the platform's testing methods and the spread of public opinion also fall within the investigation's scope. Second, whether fractures in three sample cars show a problem with that batch or a design-level common issue needs to be answered with batch, material and process data; Beijing Daily cited reports that eight other models in the same test suffered no damage with mean pedal forces above 1,600 N. Third, Dongchedi and carmakers have clashed before: during the 2023 winter test, Richard Yu publicly criticized its testing, several carmakers followed with questions, and Dongchedi then ran a second winter test — so the platform's "conflict of interest" is indeed a topic worth discussing.

Either way, whether design or manufacturing defects exist should be answered by data the investigation team releases. Nodes to watch: the investigation's conclusions and problem characterization; whether a recall is involved; regulatory measures for review platforms; and subsequent responses from the companies and the platform.

6. Frequently asked questions

What are the key dates for a Tesla–SpaceX merger?

Tesla's annual shareholder meeting is due no later than December 6; Barclays expects a merger announcement between late October and early November; the program mentioned October 21's third-quarter earnings as another window. The biggest obstacle is the shareholder vote.

Is Trump's waiver of Russia sanctions legal?

Per Reuters, the law allows the president to waive sanctions after notifying Congress that they are not in the national interest, so the dispute is mainly political. Trump has submitted the national-interest certification to Congress, drawing bipartisan criticism.

Why is the US engaging Myanmar's junta?

Mainly to ask for help shutting down scam compounds targeting Americans and to explore access to heavy rare-earth resources; obstacles include mines in armed-group-controlled areas and China as the main buyer.

Where does the Zunjie V800 case stand?

Three departments have set up a joint investigation team; Zunjie says it will cooperate fully, and conclusions have not yet been released. The company says the component passed third-party inspection, but under different conditions from Dongchedi's test.
