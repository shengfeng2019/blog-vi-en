The "Six Orders" Land at the Door: Billionaires Hunted as the Offshore-Trust Deadline Hits

On October 22, the 90-day self-review and back-tax transition period set by the Ministry of Finance and the State Taxation Administration's 2026 Notice No. 21 for overseas trusts officially expired. Bloomberg estimates that eight top billionaires — including Ding Lei (~$37 billion), Huang Zheng (~$29 billion), Lei Jun (~$20.7 billion) and Wang Ning (~$10.2 billion) — hold a combined $140.2 billion in offshore trusts across the British Virgin Islands, the Cayman Islands and elsewhere, now facing enormous back-tax pressure at a 20% rate. Officials have not named anyone as owing tax; the figures above are all Bloomberg estimates.

What sent an even deeper chill through billionaire circles: three days before the deadline (October 19), the State Taxation Administration suddenly issued a Q&A laying down a "six orders" patch on offshore trusts — financing interest not deductible, foreign corporate income tax not creditable against personal income tax, trust management fees not deductible, undistributed profits contributed before being placed into the trust fully counted into the tax base, and a sharply extended collection window for any unpaid tax above 100,000 yuan; only non-controlling employee equity incentives get a conditional exclusion. Five of the six orders are major tightenings — the knife was sharpened right at the goal line.

The show/LT's analysis: Xi Jinping's strike at the top billionaires now follows three fundamental logics. First, the treasury is desperately short of cash — the blade that started with 30-year lookbacks on companies, then 30-year lookbacks on officials, then 15-year lookbacks on social security, has finally swung at the fattest giants overseas. Second, delivering on the political banner of "common prosperity," seizing the rich to feed populist appetites. Third, the most critical signal — an excerpt of Xi's January 12 speech to the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection, published in the October 16 issue of Qiushi magazine, which in official wording stresses "preventing capital from penetrating the political sphere" and criticizes "two-faced people" (rendered by the show as "fake loyalists"). This signals that the crackdown on the rich will no longer stop at economic crimes, but will carry out political labeling and purges in the name of "capital interfering in politics" — a storm of political-business cleansing is imminent.

CSI 300 Enters a Bear Market: The Price of a Fake "Health Report"

On Thursday (October 22), the CSI 300 closed down 1.8% at 4,036.30 — 20.2% below its June peak, down ten straight weeks, officially entering a technical bear market. Bloomberg estimates $2.4 trillion in market value has evaporated (about 17 trillion yuan), erasing all gains since the September 2024 rescue and making it one of the world's worst-performing major markets.

Ironically, the National Bureau of Statistics reported on October 19 that GDP for the first three quarters grew 4.6% year-on-year (Q3 alone +4.5%) — and this "clean bill of health" became policy poison. The show quotes a Chinese economist's metaphor: the statistics bureau handed the patient a fake health report — blood sugar, cholesterol, all normal — so Chairman Xi, report in hand, "kept smoking and drinking," convinced all was well and unwilling to roll out any substantive economic support. But capital markets know exactly where the disease is; seeing the government declare "the situation is great, no major treatment needed," confidence collapsed completely and triggered a violent selloff. Separately, public reports show the "national team" — funds like Central Huijin — had already bought ETFs heavily in the first half of the year and poured in roughly $43 billion more to stabilize the market since July; but propping up a market without underlying earnings support is inherently fragile — the funds push the market up on entry and quietly slip out, leaving retail investors repeatedly trapped, with margin balances at rock bottom. Such a manufactured bull market is fragile by construction.

Shaybah Oil Field Attacked: Oil Rockets to $112, US Diplomats Pull Out

Brent crude rose about 2.9% on Thursday to roughly $112 a barrel. Saudi Arabia's energy ministry confirmed that a Houthi drone struck Saudi Aramco's Shaybah oil field; air defenses intercepted it but falling debris killed two and injured two, with field operations unaffected; there were also reports of a tanker struck and set ablaze by an unknown projectile in waters near Oman. US EIA data showed commercial crude inventories fell by 3.06 million barrels last week, with gasoline stocks sliding too.

The key driver of the geopolitical premium: the US State Department ordered non-essential personnel and families of its mission in Saudi Arabia to evacuate, and missions in other Gulf states also told families to leave. US media broadly assess this as standard defensive precaution — not preparation for a major military strike — but it also shows an inability to stop the Houthis from escalating. With the US midterms approaching and Trump with no bandwidth for the Middle East, markets fear a full Houthi assault on Saudi energy facilities — a shock far beyond an Iran blockade — and energy markets and tech stocks could swing violently in the coming days.

Gunfire Louder Beside the Negotiating Table: 21 Missiles and 630 Drones

Despite word from Trump's envoy Steve Witkoff, after meeting Putin in St. Petersburg, that "Putin agreed in principle to restart trilateral talks on November 10 (after the US midterms)," the front lines burned hotter than ever. Russia fired 21 ballistic missiles at Kyiv overnight in three waves (including 3 cluster munitions); Ukraine sent 630 drones toward Moscow in response (the figure per the Moscow mayor / Russian side). Kremlin officials revealed the meeting was short and without substance; the US plan reportedly asks Ukraine to cede all of Donbas in exchange for future NATO security guarantees — unacceptable to Kyiv — and Russia's Yuri Ushakov said the "nuances" the US brought could not fully satisfy Moscow. Short-term peace prospects look deeply pessimistic.

$4 Million to Put Your Rival's Birth Mother in an Attack Ad: Ohio's Race to the Bottom

Republican Ohio gubernatorial candidate Vivek Ramaswamy spent $4 million producing an attack ad featuring the birth mother of his Democratic rival Amy Acton — estranged for nearly 50 years — accusing her daughter of lying and untrustworthiness. Acton angrily exposed the truth: by Acton's own account, her stepfather sexually assaulted her when she was 12, and her birth mother sided with him; Acton says a court stripped her birth mother of custody in 1978; court and police records show the stepfather was charged with rape in 1978, fled, never stood trial, and died in 2004; mother and daughter have been estranged since 1978. Knowing all this and still refusing to pull the ad — with Vice President JD Vance publicly backing him, saying "you don't criticize a fellow party candidate in the heat of a campaign" — triggered fierce cross-party mainstream condemnation of their lost moral bottom line, likely inflicting long-term damage on both men's political futures.

Meituan Goes to Hong Kong to Borrow Yuan: A Privileged Arbitrage Chain

Bloomberg reported on October 21 that Meituan, with mainland approval in hand, is considering issuing offshore yuan bonds (dim sum bonds) in Hong Kong within weeks. Last year its 10-year dim sum bond carried a 3.1% coupon versus 5.125% on its dollar bonds — yet onshore loan rates are even lower and liquidity ample. So why go the long way around?

The "road to riches" revealed by the show/LT: onshore yuan cannot be freely converted under strict foreign-exchange controls, while offshore yuan in Hong Kong converts freely into dollars; companies invest the dollars in high-yield deposits above 5% or overseas M&A, then repay in Hong Kong with onshore yuan revenue when due. This model of risk-free spread arbitrage, conducted through officially blessed approvals, is out of reach for ordinary companies — exposing power rent-seeking and resource monopolies.
